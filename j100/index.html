We're running a heavy-lift drone job today. Before we talk through the aircraft, let's get the basics straight.

Job and roles: I'm the pilot in command. Our visual observer is ____. Everyone else stays clear of the takeoff, flight path and drop zone unless I say otherwise.

Public safety and exclusion zones: We fly at least 152 m (500 ft) from the general public. That includes people on foot, even when they're walking on a road; vehicles driving on a road don't count. There's a 30 m exclusion zone around the landing zone (LZ), and only crew go inside it. Crew, stay at least 5 m from the drone unless I ask you to come closer. Wherever we can, we mark the exclusion zones with cones, tape or a spotter. If anyone from the public comes near, tell me right away and we'll hold until they're clear.

No fly-over: The drone does not fly over anyone. The LZ must be clear before we approach to land.

Heads up: I'll call "heads up" on takeoff and landing. When you hear it, look up and keep clear.

Under the drone: No one under the load, ever. Keep the LZ guarded. If the load starts to swing or something looks wrong, step back and shout, don't try to catch it.

Power lines and trees: We've checked for wires and tall trees. If you see a hazard I haven't called out, stop me.

Weather: Wind and gusts have to stay under our limit. If it picks up, we pause.

Battery: We land and swap at 30%. Don't wait for a low-battery warning.

Stop the mission: Anyone can stop the job. Contact a crew member, or wave your arms so we see you. If you need me to stop, say "STOP" loud and clear.

Emergency: If the aircraft goes down or the load releases early, nobody runs in until I say it's clear.

Any questions before we walk the drone?
